      Timeline

      Phases, checkpoints & deliverables

      Phases are the work; a ◆ row is a checkpoint the plan is aiming at, between the phases rather than inside one. Drag a ⠿ grip to put either where it belongs in the sequence. Expand a phase to list what it produces — deliverables are named, not estimated, so the weeks and points stay on the phase. Reaching every checkpoint is what finishes a project; closing phases does not.

      Dependencies

      Links run project to project — which whole piece of work has to land before another can start. Order inside a project is yours to arrange; nothing checks it.

        All projects

        One column per week, labelled with the Monday it starts. The window shows up to six months at a time — pick a length, or Custom… for your own range. Each project starts folded to a single bar over its own dates, filled to how many of its deliverables are ticked; open one with the ▸ beside its name to see every phase and checkpoint. Drag a phase bar sideways to move that phase; it snaps to a week column, hold Alt to nudge it by single days. The date you are about to drop on follows the cursor, with the weekday added while Alt is held. Nothing else reschedules itself — a project waiting on this one only starts warning. Click a week number on the ruler to read that fortnight day by day; the drawer that opens is read-only.

        Not placed yet

        Projects you have estimated but not dated. Drag one onto the chart to start it that week — its undated phases are laid out back to back from there, in the order you arranged them. Phases that already have dates keep them. A drop can be undone until you reload the page.

        Cross-project dependencies

        Finish to start, in the order they were linked. A link marked V2 is one where the later project already begins before the earlier one finishes.

          Team map

          Your department at the centre, tracks around it, projects on the outside. Write a track as Source expansion / Metrics to split it into a subtrack. Circle size is total effort points, the shaded part of it is how many deliverables are ticked, and a numbered pip is the project's tier; each label shows phases done and what lands next. Dashed circles are future directions — ideas nobody has committed to yet. Point at a project to dim the map down to it and whatever it is linked to, with arrows running predecessor → successor. Click a project to open it.

            Sprint file

            The sprint file itself, edited in place. Click any block to see its markdown, click away to put it back — saving happens on its own. / on an empty block inserts one; drag ⠯ in the margin to reorder. In a table the same handle sits beside every row and above every column — drag it to move that one, click it to insert or delete there. Nothing here writes to the roadmap: a sprint that overruns is recorded in Reflection, and phase dates are changed by hand in the Project tab or not at all.
