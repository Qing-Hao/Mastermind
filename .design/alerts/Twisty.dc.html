Opening a lane loses your place — sideways, and the keyboard
laneTwisty calls renderPortfolio(), which empties the chart and builds it again. Two things that belong to the reader go with it.
The first theory was wrong, and it is worth not repeating. The guess was that chart.innerHTML = "" collapses the document height, the browser clamps scrollY, and the page ends up at the top. It does not: the browser never lays out between the clear and the rebuild in the same task, so nothing shrinks in a way anything can observe. Measured in headless Chrome at 1440×900 and 1000×720, across opening a lane, folding one, changing the window preset and collapsing every lane — vertical scroll held at 500px in every case.

Still open: “back to the top”
The original report was that the page jumps to the top. That did not reproduce, at either viewport size, on any of the four interactions — and the sidebar keeps its own scroll too.
The likeliest reading is the focus loss. With focus on <body>, the next key that moves focus starts at the top of the document and takes the view with it — which feels exactly like being sent back to the top. That is fixed. If it is still happening, it needs the specific control and what was on screen.
